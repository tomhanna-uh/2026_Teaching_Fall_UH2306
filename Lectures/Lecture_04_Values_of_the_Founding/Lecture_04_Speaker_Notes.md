## Classical liberalism

Stress: **not** modern “liberal vs conservative” party labels. Enlightenment package: limit power, expand who participates, let *individuals* and voluntary associations flourish.

### Coyne restatement
Close paraphrase of Chris Coyne (GMU): the major problem of government is to allow us to live together peacefully in a society of dignified equals. Ties directly to the course arc.

### Fulfillment restatement
**Empowering individuals to pursue their own fulfillment** — individualistic, not collectivist. Not “groups” or “the community’s fulfillment.”

Speaker language cleaned:
- Progressive / modernistic gloss on “life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.”
- Happiness here is not cheap thrills or momentary pleasure.
- Closer to finding your own highest purpose and fulfillment (Maslow / self-actualization as optional familiar reference).
- Classical liberalism is individualistic: the person, not the collective, is the unit that gets to aim at fulfillment.

## Spectrum of limits

Walk from most restrained to most expansive. This is the core of the hour.

**Thoreau:** “That government is best which governs not at all” (*Civil Disobedience*, 1849).

**“Governs least”:** From *The United States Magazine and Democratic Review*, not Thoreau. People often blur the two; Thoreau’s line is stronger. Do not misattribute on the slide.

**Krauthammer:** lowest possible level of organized violence for agreed functions.

**Constitutional limits:** force only when and how the constitution allows. **Buchanan** deferred to constitutional-politics lecture.

**Social contract table:** people give up some freedom / accept some coercion; government protects life, liberty, property. Hobbes / Locke / Rousseau as different bargains, not full theories.
